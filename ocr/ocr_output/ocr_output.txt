1.1 What is User Experience (UX) Design?

Definition:

User Experience (UX) Design is the process of designing products that provide meaningful,
relevant, and enjoyable experiences to users. It involves research, design, usability, and
emotional impact.

Example:
The ease of booking a cab on Ola or Uber shows good UX — simple steps, fast feedback, and
clear visuals.

Components of User Experience

Component Description Example

Usability How easy it is to use. Simple navigation in Gmail.
Usefulness How well it fulfills the user’s purpose. Google Maps navigation.
Desirability Emotional connection and attractiveness. Apple’s clean design.

Accessibility Usable by everyone, including disabled users. Screen readers for blind users.

Credibility Trust and reliability. Verified badges on social media.

Evolution of UX Design
1. Pre-digital Era: Focus on human factors and ergonomics.
2. Graphical User Interfaces (1980s): Apple and Xerox introduced icons and windows.
3. Web UX (1990s-2000s): Focus on usability and information architecture.

4. Mobile and Al Era (2010-Present): Personalization, AR/VR, and cross-platform design.

Elements of User Experience (Jesse James Garrett Model)

Plane Focus Example

Strategy Define business goals and user needs. “Help users book tickets easily.”
Scope Determine features and functionalities. Payment, seat selection, etc.
Structure Information and interaction design. Menu hierarchy and page flow.
Skeleton Layout and placement of elements. Buttons, forms, navigation.

Surface Final visual design. Fonts, colors, icons.

Feedback Showresponseto user action. “Item added to cart.”

User-Centric Design (UCD)

Definition:
An iterative process focused on user needs and feedback throughout design and development.

Steps:
1. Understand users
2. Design solutions
3. Test with users
4. Refine

Benefits:
Higher satisfaction, lower redesign cost, better usability.

Module 2: User Research and Journey (CO2)
2.1 Understanding Users
e Types of Users:
o Primary Users: Directly use the product.
o Secondary Users: Influence or support the primary user.
o Tertiary Users: Have an indirect interest (stakeholders).
e Problem Formulation:
o Identify user pain points.
o Define goals and challenges clearly.

e Need Finding:
Techniques to discover what users actually require.

2.2 5S Model
1. Survey: Understand the environment.
2. Segment: Divide users into groups.
3. Select: Choose target users.
4. Study: Observe and analyze their behavior.

5. Synthesize: Combine insights to form design decisions.

User Research Goals
e Understand user needs, motivations, and behaviors.
e Identify usability problems early.

e Validate assumptions.

Heuristic Analysis

A usability inspection method where experts evaluate the interface using established
principles.

Common Heuristics:
e Visibility of system status
e Match between system and real world
e User control and freedom

e Consistency and standards

User Personas

Definition:
Fictional characters representing key user types.

Example:
“Ananya, 21, college student, uses mobile apps for daily planning.”

Personas help designers empathize and design according to user goals.

Identifying & Recruiting Users
e Select users who match target demographics.

e Use surveys, social media, or user panels for recruitment.

Common Research Methods

® Module 3: UX Design Process (CO3)

3.1 Defining the UX Design Process
The UX Design Process ensures a structured way to create user-friendly designs.
Stages:

1. Research: Understand users.

2. Define: Problem statement and goals.

3. Ideate: Brainstorm possible solutions.

4. Design: Create wireframes and prototypes.

5. Test: Get user feedback.

6. Implement: Final design development.

Understanding User Needs
e Conduct surveys and interviews.
e Observe real-world user interactions.

e Use empathy maps.

Flow Chart
Visual representation of user paths within the product.
Example:

Login > Select Product > Add to Cart > Checkout > Payment Confirmation.

3.2 Empathy Map

Four Quadrants:
1. Says —What users say.
2. Thinks — What they think privately.
3. Does -—What actions they take.

4. Feels —- Emotions behind those actions.

Emotional Mapping

Understanding user emotions throughout their journey helps improve satisfaction.

4.1 Visual Design Principles

Principle Description Example

Balance = _ Even distribution of visual weight. Symmetrical layout.

4.3 Usability Testing

Definition:
Testing how easy it is for users to use the interface.

Types:

e Moderated: With facilitator guidance.

e Unmoderated: Independent user testing.

e Remote: Online.

e In-person: Physical observation.
Process:

5.1 AR (Augmented Reality) & VR (Virtual Reality)

e AR: Adds digital elements to the real world.
Example: Snapchat filters, Google Lens.

e VR: Immerses users in a completely virtual world.
Example: Oculus VR gaming, virtual tours.

UXD and XR

XR (Extended Reality):
Umbrella term covering AR, VR, and MR (Mixed Reality).

Role of UXD:
e Design intuitive 3D interactions.
e Ensure comfort and realism.

e Consider motion sickness and accessibility.

Present and Future of XR

Present:
Used in gaming, education, healthcare, and retail.

Future Trends:
e Metaverse integration
e Haptic feedback and sensory design
e Al-powered immersive experiences

e Natural user interfaces (gesture/voice-based)
